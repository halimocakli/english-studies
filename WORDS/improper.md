improper

Pronunciation

IPA (AmE): /ɪmˈprɑːpɚ/

Syllables (American): im·prop·er



---

Meanings

Part of speech: Adjective (descriptive)

Primary meaning

1. Not suitable or appropriate for a situation (often judged by social rules, etiquette, or expectations).



TR: uygunsuz, yakışıksız, münasip olmayan


Other common meanings

2. Not correct; wrong (less common, more formal).



TR: yanlış, hatalı


3. Not legal or not allowed; against rules or standards (formal/official tone).



TR: usulsüz, mevzuata aykırı, uygun olmayan


4. Not genuine / not strictly accurate (e.g., “an improper fraction” is a math term).



TR: (mat.) bileşik kesir



---

Word structure (type/grammar details)

Formation: im- (a negative prefix meaning “not”) + proper (“appropriate/correct”).

Comparatives: more improper (or improperer is rare/awkward)

Superlatives: most improper


Common patterns

It’s improper to + verb: “It’s improper to interrupt.”

improper + noun: “improper conduct / behavior / language / procedure”



---

Origin (etymology)

Latin root: proprius = “one’s own; proper; fitting.”

Proper entered English via Old French and Middle English with the sense “appropriate/correct.”

The negative prefix im- (a variant of in- meaning “not,” used before p/b/m) created improper = “not proper/appropriate.”



---

Synonyms (by sense)

1) “inappropriate / unsuitable”

inappropriate, unsuitable, unseemly, indecorous, unbecoming, tactless, ill-advised


2) “wrong / incorrect”

incorrect, wrong, inaccurate, erroneous


3) “against rules / unlawful”

improper, irregular, unauthorized, illegitimate, unlawful, unethical (depends on context)


Near-opposites (antonyms):

proper, appropriate, suitable, correct, lawful



---

Usage areas

Social etiquette & manners: improper behavior, improper remarks

Workplace/professional conduct: improper conduct, improper relationship, improper disclosure

Law/compliance/audits: improper payments, improper procedure, improper use of funds

Academic/math: improper fraction



---

Example sentences

1. Social: “It would be improper to ask her salary in a first meeting.”


2. Work: “The report flagged improper access to customer data.”


3. Rules: “Using company resources for personal business is improper.”


4. Formal ‘wrong’: “That’s an improper interpretation of the policy.”


5. Math: “ is an improper fraction.”
